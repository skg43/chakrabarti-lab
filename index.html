Research Area

Theoretical Soft, Condensed, and Biological Matter Physics

Overview

Our research sits at the interface of soft-matter physics and biological physics. In soft materials, our objectives are application-focused and aimed at addressing industrial challenges such as controlling surface migration of small molecules in complex formulations, predicting properties of confined soft matter, and quantitatively modeling corrosion protection in polymerized coatings.

In biophysics, our objectives are curiosity-driven, with a long-term goal of developing clinical applications to address current health challenges. These include modeling the mechanics of bacterial cell walls to combat antimicrobial resistance, chromosome organization in the cell nucleus and epigenetic regulation to understand abnormal genome packaging linked to disease, and biological phase separation to understand membraneless organelles and associated pathologies.

As theorists, we collaborate extensively with experimentalists across academia and industry.

Highlights

  • Soft materials: migration, confinement effects, protective coatings
  • Biological physics: cell walls, nuclear organization, phase separation
  • Theory + computation with close experimental collaboration
