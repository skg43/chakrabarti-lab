Membrane mechanics

We study how elastic and thermal fluctuations shape the behavior of membranes, from nearly planar bilayers to highly curved vesicles and tubules. Using theory and computation, we connect molecular-scale interactions to continuum descriptions (e.g., bending and tension) and quantify how geometry and material parameters control stability and shape transitions.

Vesicles under osmotic pressure

A major focus of the group is understanding how osmotic shocks and solute partitioning drive vesicle inflation, deflation, and morphological remodeling. We use coarse-grained molecular dynamics and statistical mechanics to map pressure–volume relations, characterize non-spherical shapes, and extract mechanical properties from simulation observables.

Soft matter modeling

We develop quantitative models for soft materials in complex environments, including confinement, interfaces, and multicomponent formulations. Our work emphasizes predictive, parameter-aware modeling that can be compared directly with experiments, and we collaborate closely with experimental groups in academia and industry.
